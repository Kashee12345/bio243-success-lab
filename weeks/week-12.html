Stress, Sleep and Setbacks

Protect the brain that does all your studying.

In BIO 243 around now

Spinal cord, spinal and cranial nerves (Chapter 13) and the neurological exam (Chapter 16) are often here. Late-term fatigue is real, and Exam 3 is coming.

The big idea

By now many students are tired. Work, family, other classes and A&P pile up. Sleep, food, movement and support are not extras: they are part of studying. A tired brain learns less per hour, so sleep is a study strategy.

Motivation usually follows action, not the other way around. When you do not feel like studying, shrink the task: "just ten minutes of cranial nerve flashcards." Starting is the hard part.

Setbacks happen to everyone. Talk to yourself the way you would talk to a friend who got a bad grade. And if stress starts to feel like more than stress (you cannot sleep, cannot focus, feel hopeless), reach out. USC Upstate Counseling Services is there for enrolled students and has 24/7 after-hours crisis support. You can also call or text 988 any time.

A&P in action

A ten-minute start on cranial nerves: write I to XII down the page and fill in as many names as you can. Then check (olfactory, optic, oculomotor, trochlear, trigeminal, abducens, facial, vestibulocochlear, glossopharyngeal, vagus, accessory, hypoglossal). Ten minutes often turns into thirty, but even ten counts.

Smart AI move

AI is not a counselor. If you are struggling, talk to a person: Counseling Services, your advisor, your instructor, or 988.

Check yourself

Why is sleep a study strategy?

  1. It is not; studying matters more
  2. Sleep helps your brain consolidate what you learned
  3. Sleep makes exams easier to schedule
  4. You can study while asleep

During sleep the brain strengthens and organizes new memories.

You keep putting off studying. What is the best first move?

  1. Wait until you feel motivated
  2. Shrink the task to ten minutes and start
  3. Plan a 6-hour session this weekend
  4. Skip it this week

Starting small lowers the barrier, and motivation often follows action.

Reflect

What drains your energy most right now? What is one thing you can protect this week?
