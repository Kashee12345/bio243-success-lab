Smart or Shady?

Know where the line is, in BIO 243 and in every other class.

In BIO 243 around now

The central nervous system (Chapter 13: brain regions, meninges, spinal cord) and the neurological exam (Chapter 16) are usually here. Lots of anatomy terms, and lots of temptation to let AI "just summarize it."

The big idea

USC Upstate leaves many AI decisions to each instructor, so the rules can change from one class to the next. In BIO 243 they are clear: AI is fine for studying, not for graded work, quizzes or exams. Presenting AI work as your own is an academic integrity violation under the syllabus.

Some situations are not obvious. Can you use AI to check grammar in a CircleIn post? To make flashcards from your own notes? To explain a Reading Guide question you are stuck on? When the answer depends on the class, check the syllabus or ask before you do it. Asking is never a problem; guessing can be.

Privacy is part of this too. Never paste in your student ID, grades, health details, or a classmate's name or work.

A&P in action

Smart: "Make me a practice quiz on the four brain regions from my notes below" (with your own notes pasted in). Shady: pasting your Blackboard quiz questions in while the quiz is open. Check first: using AI to reword your own lab write-up in another course. The difference is whether AI is helping you learn or doing graded work for you.

Smart AI move

Rule 5: every class is different. When in doubt, ask before you use it, and if AI is allowed on an assignment, say how you used it.

Check yourself

Your instructor in another course allows AI for brainstorming. What should you do in BIO 243?

  1. Use it for Guided Practice too
  2. Follow the BIO 243 rules: no AI on graded work unless the assignment says so
  3. Use it only on quizzes
  4. Assume all courses allow it

Each course sets its own rules. BIO 243's rules apply in BIO 243.

Which of these is safe to paste into an AI tool?

  1. Your student ID and grades
  2. A classmate's discussion post
  3. Your own notes on the meninges, with no personal details
  4. A screenshot of an open quiz

Your own study notes without personal information are fine. The others break privacy or integrity rules.
